Salvador Xiongfei Wang

Welcome. I am a doctoral student at the Graduate School of International Cultural Studies, Tohoku University, in Sendai, Japan.

I was born in Shijiazhuang, Hebei, China. After completing my bachelor's degree at Beijing International Studies University, I lived in Mexico City for a decade working in ICT and international tourism. With the four major languages I often use in work and study (Chinese, English, Spanish, and Japanese), I believe there must be some universal principles in language learning, and I am still seeking the answer.

My research interests focus on second language acquisition, especially on how students learn spoken vocabulary more efficiently and with greater retention, as well as the underlying mechanisms. My work combines classroom experiments with computational modeling and computer-assisted language learning. I have also studied and worked in psycholinguistics (Chinese-Japanese bilingual processing), cognitive psychology (skill acquisition theory), and neurolinguistics (declarative / procedural memory).
